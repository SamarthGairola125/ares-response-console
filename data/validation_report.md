## 5b. Fleet reduction (under 30 aerostats per scenario)

Camera detection now falls with slant range (Johnson criteria: a 0.75 m person needs ≈0.75 resolved cycles for 50 % detection; 2,560 effective pixels across the field of view). Per-scenario height, lens field of view and coverage rule were chosen by `tests/fleet_search.mjs` / `fleet_search2.mjs` as the smallest fleet that keeps the share of people found at the previous level. Means over seeds 1–5; "previous" is the published v4 configuration with the earlier camera model (no loss with height), "new" uses the stricter camera model.

Assam (−1.7 points) and Nepal (−1.2 points) find slightly fewer people than before over five seeds; the other three find as many or more. Assam was previously over budget (39 aerostats, coverage target not met) and is now 29 with higher weighted coverage. The fleet limit of 29 includes up to 3 aerostats added at replanning. Settings were selected on seed 7 and checked on seeds 1–5, so they are tuned to this simulation, not validated in the field.

## 6. Population estimator (stage 10) — bias and the dispersed-population assumption

The synthetic ground truth places 15 % of people uniformly over land (road users, field workers). The real-data prior is residential, so without an allowance for them the estimator is biased low. v4 adds an explicit 15 % dispersed share to the **estimator prior only** (placement is unchanged).

| Scenario | Error, no allowance | Error, 15 % allowance | Seeds 1–5 (15 %) | ±90 % interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| dehradun | -24.2 % | -15.8 % | -9.8, -16, -14.9, -19.4, -16.4 % | ±10 |
| wayanad | -30.4 % | -18.9 % | -16.2, 2.9, -10.5, -7.6, -8.1 % | ±22 |
| assam | 4.7 % | 5.9 % | 2.7, 2.9, 4.8, 4.4, 5.2 % | ±20 |
| delhi | -1.9 % | -1.2 % | -3.5, 0.4, -1.2, -3.5, -1.9 % | ±19 |
| nepal | -20.9 % | -11 % | -12.2, -6.5, -4.4, -8.6, -4.3 % | ±24 |

Remaining bias in the mountain debris scenarios (-19.4 % to +2.9 % across seeds) comes from people the cameras cannot see (canopy, debris, terrain shadow) while the cell-level detection probability is evaluated at the cell centre. The reported ±90 % interval is far narrower than the actual error there, so the interval is **not calibrated** for those scenarios. This is a model limitation to state in the paper, not a data problem.
